The machines and the work

PDPs at universities

For about fifteen years, the DEC PDP line was the working medium of academic computing. Cheap enough for a single department and interactive enough to sit at, the PDPs became the machines on which an extraordinary amount of foundational software was first written. Time-sharing, Unix, Lisp and artificial intelligence, interactive graphics, the whole hacker culture, all of it has a PDP somewhere near its origin. Spacewar! on MIT's PDP-1 is the first of these, and far from the last.

The reading

The medium of a generation's computing

It is hard to overstate how much of modern computing was first written on these machines. The PDP line was not a backdrop to this work; it was its medium, the constitutive ground on which time-sharing, Unix, Lisp and interactive graphics became thinkable and then real. Spacewar! belongs to the very start of that story, the moment a roomful of students discovered that an interactive computer was a thing you could do something playful and expressive with.
