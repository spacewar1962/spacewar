# Margaret Masterman and the military funding of the CLRU

Research summary, compiled 2026-06-17. Background note for the Cold War machine-translation context surrounding early computational work. Citations below are from secondary literature and must be verified against Zotero before entering any draft.

## Summary

Margaret Masterman founded the Cambridge Language Research Unit (CLRU) in 1955–56 (some accounts trace an informal start to 1953) and directed it for roughly two decades as a machine-translation and computational-linguistics laboratory. Crucially, the CLRU sat *outside* the official structures of Cambridge University. It had no university line-funding and was, by its own accounts, "extremely equipment-poor," so Masterman financed it almost entirely from external grants. A large share of those grants came from the United States military.

## Funding sources

The secondary literature consistently reports the same set of funders. The CLRU was supported by three US agencies, two of them military:

- **AFOSR**, the US Air Force Office of Scientific Research (the Air Force's external-research funding arm)
- **ONR**, the US Office of Naval Research
- **NSF**, the (civilian) US National Science Foundation

alongside:

- **OSTI**, the UK Office for Scientific and Technical Information
- some Canadian government support
- later, **European Commission / EU funds** administered from Luxembourg

## Why a Cambridge lab drew US military money

This was standard Cold War machine-translation economics rather than an anomaly. After 1954 the US defence establishment invested heavily in machine translation, overwhelmingly to obtain fast, rough Russian-to-English translation of scientific and military documents. The Air Force and Navy funded competent MT groups wherever they existed, including in Britain. The CLRU's interlingua-and-thesaurus approach made it an attractive recipient. The unit survived for twenty years on this patronage precisely because domestic British support for language-processing research was so hard to secure.

## Caveat on specifics

The accessible secondary sources name the *agencies* but do not quote specific contract numbers, grant numbers, or amounts. Those figures exist in the primary record, in the CLRU's own technical reports filed with the sponsors and in the corresponding DTIC and NSF deposit records, but they are not reproduced in the readily available histories.

Note also that the frequently repeated "£250 from six supporters" figure (Wikipedia, CLRU entry) is *not* the military money. That was the small seed sum used to rent the unit's premises ("Adie's Museum," Millington Road, Cambridge). It should not be confused with the AFOSR / ONR / NSF grants.
