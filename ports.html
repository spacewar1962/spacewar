Ports and clones

The long journey, beyond the PDP-1

Spacewar! did not stay on one machine. Through the 1960s and into the 1970s it was carried, re-coded, and reinvented on computers across dozens of labs, often rebuilt from memory or a magazine listing rather than the MIT source. Each port is a re-reading of the game: a decision about what Spacewar! essentially is, made by someone who usually never saw the original code. The fullest census is the Kinephanos study "Space Odyssey: the long journey of Spacewar! from MIT to computer labs around the world" (2015), summarised below and expanded with additions recovered by Berry and Marino in 2026.

Five ports, read closely

Some of these ports survive well enough, in a source listing, a magazine write-up, or a recovered printout, to be read in their own right. Each is a piece of design criticism in code: a re-implementer's argument about what the game should be.

  • Cambridge: Duel on the PDP-7 · Peterson and Viner's "viscous space", 1968, and the Margaret Masterman connection.
  • Minnesota: invisibility instead of luck · Kuhfeld's CDC 3100 version and its 1971 Analog write-up.
  • Stanford: Gorin's five-ship game · the PDP-10 version played at the 1972 Olympics, and Alan Kay's "build your own".
  • MIT-AI: the self-documenting SPCWAR · the ITS lineage that wrote its own version history into its comments.
  • PDP-11: Bryant, Seiler and the GT40 · the 1974 DECUS version lost and recovered from a printout.
